Why a pendulum's period depends only on length

The period

For small swings, a pendulum of length L (pivot to the centre of the bob) has a period T = 2π √(L / g). A 25 cm pendulum swings back and forth in about 1.00 s. Because of the square root, you need four times the length to double the period: 10 cm takes 0.63 s and 40 cm takes 1.27 s (and a 1 m pendulum about 2 s).

Why mass doesn't matter

A heavier bob feels a bigger pull from gravity, but it is also harder to accelerate by exactly the same factor, so the two cancel. That's the same reason a heavy and a light ball fall together. Try the mass slider: the period doesn't change. Mass only matters once air resistance is included, and then a heavier bob keeps swinging for longer.

Big swings are a little slower

The simple formula assumes small angles. At 20° the real period is about 0.8% longer, and at 60° about 7% longer. The simulation solves the full equation, so its measured period matches the "period at this angle" readout.

Why real pendulums slow down, and why clocks have weights

A real pendulum loses a little energy on every swing. Air pushes back on the moving bob (drag) and the pivot rubs, and both turn some of the swing's energy into heat. With less energy the bob can't climb as high, so each swing gets a little smaller until it stops. Turn on air damping to see this. A heavier bob of the same size keeps going for longer, because the same drag takes a smaller share of its larger store of energy. A pendulum clock solves the problem with a weight on a chain (or a wound spring). As the weight slowly falls, the clock's escapement gives the pendulum a tiny push each swing, just enough to replace what drag and friction took. Because the period depends on length and hardly at all on swing size, these pushes keep it going without changing the time it keeps.

Check it against a real one

Calibrate the screen, tie a small weight to a thread and measure the length from your fingers to the middle of the weight (up to 40 cm). Set the same length, hold your fingers at the drawn pivot and release together. The view grows down the page to fit the whole pendulum at true size, so scroll down to follow a long one.
