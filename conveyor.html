Conveyor speed, gaps and free time

Products arrive at a fixed rate. Run the belt faster and the products spread out: the gap between them grows, and so does the time a fixed point on the belt line sits empty between products, even though the rate hasn't changed.

The maths

  5. free = gap / v = T − l / v –

Same rate, different belt speeds

The rate fixes the timing

If products arrive at a fixed rate, one passes any point on the line every T = 60 / rate seconds, whatever the belt speed. At 120 per minute that's one every 0.5 s.

The belt speed sets the spacing

In that time the belt moves v × T, so that's the pitch: the distance from one product to the next. A faster belt spreads the same products further apart, and the gap between them is p − l. Run the belt at exactly l / T and the gap disappears: the products touch, and that's the slowest the belt can go at this rate.

Free time at a fixed point

Watch one spot on the line. Each product covers it for l / v seconds, then it's empty for gap / v until the next one arrives. The two always add up to T. Speeding the belt up doesn't change T, but each product passes in less time, so more of every cycle is free.

For example, at 120 per minute with 100 mm products: at 0.25 m/s the pitch is 125 mm, the gap 25 mm, and the point is free for only 0.1 s of each 0.5 s. At 1 m/s the pitch is 500 mm, the gap 400 mm, and it's free for 0.4 s. Same rate, four times the free time. That's why a faster belt can give a sensor, gate or gripper more time between products, not less.
