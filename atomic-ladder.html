The pixel at the small end of the scale

Start with one real pixel

On a calibrated screen the journey starts at true size: one pixel, about 0.277 mm across on a 24-inch 1080p monitor. A typical human hair, 70 µm thick, is about 4 times thinner. Each step down then magnifies the view, until one pixel covers less than the width of an atom, and finally less than a proton.

Atoms across this pixel

In a silicon crystal the atoms sit on average 272 pm apart along any line (half the measured lattice parameter, 543.1 pm, because each cubic cell holds 8 atoms). So a row of about 1.02 million silicon atoms would span one pixel of a 24-inch 1080p screen. The readout uses your own screen. As you zoom in, the count falls, until one silicon atom spans many pixels.

Below an atom: sizes become limits

The gold atom is drawn 452 pm across, the size of its electron cloud (where the density falls to 0.001 electrons per cubic bohr). Its nucleus is 10.9 fm across, about 41,600 times smaller, and a proton is 1.68 fm. Electrons and quarks are different: no experiment has measured any size for them. They may be true points. All we have are upper limits: an electron's radius is under 2.8 × 10−19 m and a quark's under 4.3 × 10−19 m, so the last stop shows a circle that a quark, if it has any size at all, must fit inside.

The pixel at both ends

This journey covers about 17 powers of ten below one pixel. The space page runs the other way: from the same pixel up until the whole Milky Way, 100,000 light-years across, fits inside one. Your pixel is about 15 powers of ten bigger than the quark-size limit, and about 25 powers of ten smaller than the Milky Way.

What is approximate

Living things vary: the hair, blood cell, bacterium and virus are typical sizes from the cited ranges, and their shapes are simple outlines. The virus is SARS-CoV-2, drawn at the middle of its measured range. The atom and nucleus have no sharp edge, so the drawn edges are definitions (a density contour, and twice the rms charge radius). The objects are lined up side by side for comparison; only the gold nucleus is placed where it really is, at the centre of its atom. The electron and quark markers show where to look, not a size.
