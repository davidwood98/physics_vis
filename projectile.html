The maths

Horizontal and vertical motion are independent. Your current values plugged in (g = 9.81 m/s²).

  1. vx = v cos θ,  vy = v sin θ –
  2. x = vx t,  y = h0 + vy t − ½ g t2 –
  3. H = h0 + vy2 / 2g –
  4. R = v2 sin 2θ / g  (from the ground) –

Projectile motion, explained

Two motions at once

Once a ball leaves your hand (ignoring air), nothing pushes it sideways, so it moves horizontally at a steady vx = v cos θ. Vertically, gravity slows it on the way up and speeds it up on the way down, exactly as if it had been thrown straight up at vy = v sin θ. Put the two together and you get a parabola.

Range and the 45° rule

Thrown from the ground, the ball lands at R = v2 sin 2θ / g. sin 2θ is largest at 45°, so that's the angle for maximum range, and angles either side of 45° (say 30° and 60°) land in the same place. A 10 m/s throw at 45° travels 10.2 m, reaches 2.55 m high and is in the air for 1.44 s. Range grows with the square of speed: throw twice as fast and it goes four times as far.

Reading the view

A real throw is far bigger than any screen, so the view is scaled to fit the whole flight, with the scale bar in the corner and a 1.8 m person beside the launch point for comparison. Time isn't scaled: at 1× the ball takes exactly as long as a real throw would.

Real balls fall short

Air resistance slows real balls, so they land shorter than this and their best angle is a little below 45°. For a slow, heavy ball over a short distance the difference is small.
